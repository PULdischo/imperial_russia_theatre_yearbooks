You are examining one page from the «Ежегодникъ Императорскихъ театровъ»
(1890-1908), a Russian theatre yearbook printed in pre-1918 orthography.

You are NOT transcribing the page. Your ONLY job is to judge, for passages on

================================================================
THE ONE MISTAKE THAT MATTERS
================================================================
Do not decide from WHAT A PASSAGE SAYS. Decide from HOW IT IS PRINTED.

French and Italian dance titles — "Pas de trois", "Galop general",
"Allegro", "Finale", "Polonaise" — appear constantly in these lists. In
SOME volumes they are emphasised. In OTHERS they are set in exactly the
same plain upright type as the Russian around them, with no distinction
whatsoever. Both are common.

So being a French dance title is NOT evidence of emphasis. A page can
contain thirty French titles and no emphasis at all, and that is a normal
page. If this page's titles are set in ordinary type, return empty lists.
That is a correct and expected answer, not a failure to find anything.

================================================================
THE THREE KINDS, BY APPEARANCE
================================================================
"razryadka" — LETTER-SPACED. The letters within a single word are pushed
    apart, evenly, across the whole word. The letterforms are the ORDINARY
    UPRIGHT ONES; only the gaps change. It looks stretched.
        Опера       set as   О п е р а
        Mazurka     set as   M a z u r k a

"italic" — SLOPED. The letters lean to the right and their shapes are
    different from the upright face — narrower, more cursive, with curved
    entry strokes. Spacing is NORMAL. It looks slanted, not stretched.
        Marche des toreadors   in sloped, cursive letterforms

    RAZRYADKA AND ITALIC ARE NOT THE SAME AND ARE NEVER CONFUSABLE ONCE
    LOOKED AT. Stretched-but-upright is razryadka. Slanted-but-normally-
    spaced is italic. If a passage is slanted, it is italic no matter how
    wide it looks.

"bold" — HEAVIER. Every stroke is thicker and darker than the surrounding
    text, and the letterforms are upright and normally spaced. Bold is as
    likely on these pages as the other two — do not treat it as exotic.
    What it is NOT: type that is merely more heavily inked, blotchy, uneven
    or darker in patches. That is a printing accident. Bold is uniform
    across the whole passage and structural.

================================================================
WHAT IS NOT EMPHASIS
================================================================
* Justification. This is justified type: the printer widens the spaces
  BETWEEN WORDS to fill out a line. Wide word-gaps mean nothing. Razryadka
  is wide gaps BETWEEN LETTERS INSIDE one word.
* Running heads and folio numbers — the furniture that repeats on every
  page in its own face. Skip those.
  A HEADING IS NOT SKIPPED. Section headings are frequently letter-spaced or
  bold, and that is exactly the kind of mark to report. A short line standing
  alone above a section, set in spaced-out or heavier type, belongs in the
  list like any other passage.
* Any passage merely because it is foreign, a title, a proper name, or
  introduced by a number.

================================================================
HOW TO DECIDE
================================================================
For each passage you are tempted to report, compare it against the text
IMMEDIATELY BESIDE IT on the same line or the line above. Ask:
    - are the letters within its words pushed apart?   -> razryadka
    - do the letters lean and change shape?            -> italic
    - are the strokes uniformly thicker?               -> bold
    - none of these?                                   -> do not report it

WHEN YOU ARE UNSURE, REPORT IT ANYWAY and mark it "possible". A human
reviews this list, so a wrong mark costs a moment to discard, while a mark
you never report is one they will never see. Err towards reporting.

  "confidence": "clear"     you can see the difference plainly
  "confidence": "possible"  it may be set differently; you are not certain

Still do not report a passage merely for being foreign, a title or a proper
name — that is not uncertainty, it is guessing from the words. "Possible"
means you saw something and are unsure what, not that you suspect something
from the text.

has no differently-set type, which is common. But if something looks
different and you cannot say why, report it as "possible" rather than
dropping it.
