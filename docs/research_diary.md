# Research Diary — Imperial Theater Yearbooks Project

> **Generated by AI.** This log was written by Claude (an AI assistant),
> not by the project's researcher, summarizing work done in each session.

A plain-language log of work on turning 144 scanned volumes of the
*Ежегодникъ Императорскихъ театровъ* (Yearbook of the Imperial
Theaters, 1890/91–1907/08) into a searchable dataset. Written for
someone with no technical background. Dates are reconstructed from the
project's internal logs and file timestamps — flag anything that looks
wrong.

---

## July 27, 2026 — First full transcription pass

- Ran the first full transcription pass across all 144 scanned volumes
  (1,299 pages), covering every category: administrators, ballet
  dancers, musicians, production staff, theater school staff, graduates.
- All 1,299 pages transcribed successfully.
- Accuracy check against a small set of hand-transcribed sample pages:
  roughly 83–96% depending on category.

## July 28, 2026 — Date and performance-status fixes

- Fixed missing dates caused by abbreviated Russian month names (e.g.
  "Февр." for February) not being recognized. Date fill rate went from
  89% to nearly 100%.
- Fixed a bug where performances dated with a season-spanning year range
  (e.g. "1896–1897") were always assigned to the first year, even when
  the actual month fell in the second year — a silent one-year
  mis-dating.
- Added a check that flags, for each theater and date, whether an
  expected performance record is actually present or missing.

## August 15, 2026 — Full re-run; found a 10-season gap

- Re-ran the full transcription from scratch, in one sitting.
- Found that 10 of 18 seasons of the Repertoire (performance and
  box-office) category had been silently skipped in an earlier run.
- Cause: those files were stored as iCloud placeholders (not fully
  downloaded to the laptop), and the system skipped them without
  raising an error.
- Re-downloaded and re-processed the missing seasons. Final total:
  1,349 pages.

## August 17, 2026 — Checked the accuracy-scoring method itself

- Investigated the script used to score transcription accuracy, not the
  underlying data.
- Found it compared entries by their position in a list rather than by
  identity — one extra or missing name partway down a page threw off
  every comparison after it.
- About half of all flagged "errors" turned out to be this artifact, not
  real mistakes.
- Corrected error rate: about 1.9%, not the roughly 14% the flawed
  method had suggested.

## August 18, 2026 — Manual verification against original scans

- Checked 99 entries where a performance-credit tally didn't sum
  correctly, against the original scanned pages.
  - About half were genuine arithmetic errors in the original printed
    yearbook, not transcription errors.
  - The other half were real transcription errors, now logged
    separately.
- Reviewed 54 pairs of same-named people flagged as possible duplicates
  (same name, different years — same person or two different people?),
  checking service dates for each before deciding.
  - One pair was initially approved, then reversed after the evidence
    proved too thin.
- Found that roughly 130 Musicians entries were cross-references (e.g.
  "see Opera Orchestra") rather than full listings, and were
  disconnected from the person's full record elsewhere. Began
  reconnecting them.

## August 19, 2026 (today) — Reconnected split records; two dancers, one name

- Finished reconnecting the disconnected musician cross-references to
  their full records — about 27 people, each verified against service
  dates and, where the spelling was ambiguous, the original scanned
  page.
- Caught and corrected several cases where the record-merging process
  assigned the wrong name to a combined record.
- Ran a full check for stray or non-name text sitting in name fields.
  Found several errors, including a footnote symbol that had been
  captured as if it were a person.
- Found 14 records with a second name in parentheses (stage name,
  maiden name, "also known as").
  - Most were one real person whose record had been split apart across
    the dataset and needed reuniting — including a dancer who performed
    under two alternating stage names, "Петропавловская" and "Эльпе,"
    with records scattered under both.
  - One case went the other way: "Гаврилова (Воскресенская)" turned out
    to be **two different dancers who both happened to be named Evdokia
    Gavrilova** — one in service since 1872 (patronymic Ivanovna), the
    other since 1884 (patronymic Pompeevna, and only she used the stage
    name Voskresenskaya). The yearbook itself distinguishes them as
    "Гаврилова 1-я" / "Гаврилова 2-я." Kept as two separate records
    rather than merged.
